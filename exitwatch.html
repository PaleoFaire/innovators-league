PRE-COMPANY DEALFLOW · SEC FORM D

Exit Watch

Every other page here points inward, at companies we already track. This one points outward and asks the opposite question: which company is about to exist that we have never heard of?

How this works

You cannot reliably detect the departure. LinkedIn has no lawful API and scraping it is brittle and against their terms, so we do not try. We detect the formation instead, which announces itself in public records.

Every US private raise over $5,000 files an SEC Form D within fifteen days of first sale — weeks or months before any press release. The filing names the new entity, its industry, the year it was incorporated, the capital actually sold, and its officers, by name. We sweep the whole EDGAR firehose daily, drop the pooled funds and real-estate vehicles that make up two thirds of it, keep newly incorporated frontier-adjacent entities we do not already track, and match the officers against the founders already documented in our own database.

What a row here is, and is not. A name on a Form D matching a name in our database is a hypothesis, not a fact. Names collide. We never assert that a named individual did anything — we tell you what the filing says, what our database says, and how well the two corroborate. Every row links straight to the filing so you can check it in one click.

Nothing matches those filters.

About this data. Source is the SEC EDGAR daily index for Form D, an official US government record, swept every morning. Filings from entities already in COMPANIES are suppressed — a tracked company raising capital is not a new formation, and surfacing one as though it were would be the most embarrassing thing this tool could do. Coverage is US-only; a company incorporated in Delaware but operating abroad appears, a company that never raises US capital does not. Form D is filed after first sale, so this is early relative to press coverage, not relative to the round itself.
